# Heartlet — Art Brief (round nbp2)
Meaning: a lover asking to be written into someone else's story ("May I be in your fable"). She is the author/heroine drifting in her own tale; the viewer is the would-be knight at the bank. Courtly love = devotion that waits outside the frame.

## Precedents
- Millais, *Ophelia* (1851–52, Tate N01506): flowers chosen for meaning — poppy=death/sleep, violets (necklace)=faithfulness/early death, forget-me-nots, pansy=love in vain, daisy=innocence, rose (cheek)=youth/love, willow=forsaken love, nettle=pain, meadowsweet=uselessness. Painted from the real Hogsmill river, every plant in season. https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/millais-ophelia-n01506
- Hamlet IV.vii (Gertrude: "There is a willow grows aslant a brook… crow-flowers, nettles, daisies, and long purples… Her clothes spread wide, and mermaid-like awhile they bore her up"). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophelia
- Waterhouse Ophelias (1889, 1894, 1910) — she sits on willow over lily pads; muted greens, white/blue dress. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophelia_(Waterhouse_paintings)
- Gothic/sublime: Grimshaw moonlit lanes (single moon, wet reflections, gold window glow) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Atkinson_Grimshaw ; Friedrich *Abbey in the Oakwood* (ruined gothic arch) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Abbey_in_the_Oakwood ; Böcklin *Isle of the Dead*; Doré engravings; Pre-Raphaelite glazing.

## Deliberate choices
- Flowers (4 only, all real pond/stream-margin plants that float or drift): **forget-me-not** (Myosotis scorpioides — grows at water margins; "write me in, don't forget"), **pansy/viola** loose blossoms ("love in vain" — the unanswered ask), **a single red rose** dropped by the suitor (courtly token, the only warm accent), **white water lily** (Nymphaea alba) with ONE pad lower-left (the only rooted plant; stems run straight down into dark water). Plus a few willow leaves (forsaken love). Cut flowers float loose with no roots; nothing grows out of the water except the lily.
- Light: single source upper right (sun or moon). Shadows fall lower-left; specular glints only on wet skin/hair/ripple crests facing the source.
- Ripples: her raised hand just touched her brow; drips from the wrist make 2–3 concentric rings spreading from near her right-of-image shoulder; rings bend around hair/floating fabric and are interrupted by obstacles; elsewhere the surface is still glass reflecting sky.
- Wet cotton/linen: clings and turns translucent where it touches skin (collarbone, shoulder, upper chest crests), with fine tension wrinkles radiating from contact points; where air is trapped below the waterline the fabric balloons into soft domes; the hem/sleeve edges float flat and dark-edged. No sculpted or "plastic" folds.
- Moonlight: low saturation, blue shift, rods-only vision → indigo/blue-grey values, pale cool skin, highlights small and sharp; one warm accent (rose) allowed.
