# Went the Day Well? — Artometrics project brief
Saved November 7, 2026 (Pacific Time).

## Creative intent and decisions
Develop an interpretation inspired by the 1942 film. Translate its felt impact for contemporary viewers, rather than merely updating costumes, dialogue, gore, or technology. A present-day setting is a proposal, not locked.

The user's Nolan opera/Zorro analogy is a useful conceptual model: translating an experience across media can require changing its representation. The attribution and precise original explanation have not been verified.

Target: a compact, prestige thriller with emotional variety, dark humor, suspense, intimacy, and sudden consequential violence. Hospitality should be capable of becoming menace. Avoid a literal imitation of Tarantino; use broad dramatic tools in an original voice.

Approved storyboard direction: Gumball-inspired mixed media, with coherent geography and performances. Fictional graphic violence can be used, but should serve character, consequence, and aftermath rather than escalation for its own sake.

## Scene audit method
Audit the correct film or a reliable script before writing new dialogue or producing new visuals. For each scene record:
- What each person wants; what they say, conceal, and do.
- What the audience knows versus each character's knowledge.
- The changing level of suspicion, trust, collaboration, and resistance.
- Expectation, reversal, consequence, and what carries into the next scene.
- Deliberate subtext versus our interpretation or a historical assumption.
- Geography, exits, threatening sounds, performance, duration, and opportunities for restraint.

Quiet scenes earn their place by changing relationships; violence earns its place by changing consequences. Preserve distinctive, funny, imperfect villagers before treating them as resistance fighters.
